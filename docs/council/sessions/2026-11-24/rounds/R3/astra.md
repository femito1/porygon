## B4: Train a tactical residual from paired terminal outcomes  —  WITHDRAW

- response to critiques: A and C are right that I skipped the key measurement: whether a player-visible position predicts a reproducible action advantage. Sharing the opponent’s first choice does not keep later RNG draws aligned. At eight replicates, a standard error near 0.22 can swamp a 0.02–0.05 edge at one root. Noise at one root does not prove learning across roots impossible, but `dg-02` and `dg-03` supply loss associations, not evidence of a learnable advantage. C also correctly distinguishes the entity-pointer head from a fixed action vector: a win-probability difference has no justified conversion to a policy-logit change at my proposed scale. `ns-27` and `ns-28` are warnings about changing the policy, not refutations of terminal labels. I retain state cloning only as a diagnostic: clone both first-person trackers as well as the simulator, and check whether an action advantage selected on one batch of continuations repeats on independent continuations from held-out games. No residual training on the present evidence.

## B5: Constrain simulated replies using the opponent’s own view  —  REVISE

- response to critiques: A4 is the right cheap comparison, but its opponent-action head sees **our** private observation. It tests whether a generic reply prior helps; it cannot establish whether concealing our bench from that prior helps. C is right that `battle.team` cannot reconstruct public history, that prior temperature must be fixed, and that the margin needs recalibration. The constraint can raise the value of a fixed strategy without necessarily widening the gap between two actions. I withdraw the immediate 1,000-pair slot request and the 4–6 day estimate.
- revised proposal:
  - lever: search
  - generations: gen9
  - mechanism: Track the public protocol from turn one. In each sampled world, combine that history with the simulated opponent’s own team and private request-equivalent information. Use B1-compatible weights so it can see its own Tera type. Map its T 0.5 policy to legal replies; constrain half the reply probability to that distribution and let the solver place the rest. Keep one strategy for us across worlds and hold forced-switch handling identical across arms.
  - evidence for: `solve_bayes` minimizes separately in each world against matrices built with our actual private team. The unrestricted reply can therefore punish a coverage move even when nothing public distinguishes it from a harmless alternative. Actual two-sided fastenv traces provide a reference for testing the reconstructed view.
  - evidence against / prior attempts: `fpr-307072` scored 40.8% when sampling our root play; `fpr-307076` found no gain from tree regret matching. Neither tested this constraint, but both caution against assuming a more elaborate solver plays better. Half the reply remains privileged, and the frozen net may poorly predict an independent opponent.
  - predicted effect: **+0.6 points [−1.5, +2.0]** over otherwise matched lookahead2 at fixed 200k. Only changed commitments can help; a misplaced prior can suppress the correct tactical warning.
  - cost: About one engineering day for the A4-head comparison; another 6–10 days for public tracking and parity only if warranted. Budget 2–4 Dojo T4-hours and 8–16 CPU-hours offline. No A30 use or initial slot reservation.
  - kill test: First compare the A4-head prior and the actual opposite-seat policy on 2,000 fresh, held-out fastenv roots, one per game, predicting executed decisions of held-out policy checkpoints from their pre-action views. Use the actual legal options and the same move/switch mapping for both. Build the tracker only if the opposite-seat prior improves mean log loss with a paired 95% interval excluding zero. Then require parity with the actual opposite seat on 300 replayed battles, including Illusion and first-reveal ordering; perturbing our unrevealed bench or unused Tera must leave its prior unchanged. On the 1,403-position study, calibrate margins on one game-disjoint split and evaluate on the other. At matched override rate and with the same constrained solver, require improvement over the cheaper A4-head prior in K32-confirmed override precision, with a paired 95% lower bound above zero. Otherwise park.
  - scale-up if GO: Freeze the solver, temperature, margin and switch handling. Use a variance-sized duplicate-pair comparison against the cheaper prior, then compare both against independent pure search and fixed-200k Foul Play on matched fresh pairs. No mixture sweep based on those games.
  - failure modes / confounds: First-person parity in the true world does not validate the sampled-world distribution. Better reply prediction need not improve minimax decisions. B1 and any C2 switch changes must be the same in both search arms.
  - formal check: For a legal prior p, the feasible replies are exactly the distributions y satisfying yⱼ ≥ 0.5pⱼ: the remainder z = 2y − p is nonnegative and sums to one. Thus the existing solver can use transformed column j equal to `0.5 M[:,j] + 0.5 M p`. This establishes solver equivalence, not a win-rate guarantee.
