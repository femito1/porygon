## B1: Give the policy its own Tera type before it chooses whether to Tera

- lever: data
- generations: gen9
- mechanism: Read each friendly Pokémon’s `teraType` from its owner’s Showdown request, match by `ident`, and populate the existing `tera` and `tera_p` inputs in both encoders. Keep “has Terastallized” separate from “knows its Tera type.” The distinction matters: the pinned set library contains otherwise identical Volcarona sets with Fiery Dance, Giga Drain, Quiver Dance and Tera Blast, one Tera Ground and one Tera Water. The same four moves call for different decisions. This information is available against every opponent.
- evidence for: The [pinned simulator](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/7b02eb39878f4b486f658a752b38c161379bd352/sim/pokemon.ts) sends both fields. `search.request_tera` already reads the type; `v2.encoder._encode_own` reads a different field, and `fastenv/js/encoder_v2.js` explicitly documents that it stays empty until use. `v2/damage.py` also gates the hypothetical Tera attacker on a nonzero type. The relevant baselines are raw 60.8% (`g9q-32`) and lookahead2 61.6% (`ns-32`), both against fixed 200k.
- evidence against / prior attempts: The logged policy already Terastallized in 90.3% of self-play games, without an obvious wasted-Tera signature (`dg-02`, associated loss report). Frequency does not test whether it chose correctly *conditional on the type*. Engine search already receives the type, so I expect less gain on top of lookahead2.
- predicted effect: I infer **+1.5 points raw, range −0.5 to +4**, versus a matched control at fixed 200k; **+0.5 to +2** is my working upside with lookahead2. The raw midpoint requires changing the outcome of three games per 200. Species and moves often narrow the type already; Volcarona shows why they cannot always recover it.
- cost: Estimate 0.5–1 engineering day; 12–15 A40 GPU-hours and 120–150 CPU-hours including both training arms. Use the existing A40 allocation in place; no change to the A30 run.
- kill test: Pre-register a request-to-tensor check on both sides of 100 fresh duplicate pairs; stop if the deployed encoder already supplies the correct type. Otherwise fork the same uncooled checkpoint twice, 25M steps plus identical 5M cooldowns. Only the candidate’s owner-visible observation changes; keep auxiliary labels and frozen opponents’ encoders fixed. Play candidate versus control in-process, T 0.5, 4,000 fresh duplicate pairs. GO iff the pair-mean 95% lower bound exceeds 50%; otherwise park.
- scale-up if GO: Repeat from another training seed. Compare both arms on the same 2,000 fresh Showdown pairs per arm against fixed-200k Foul Play; report the paired difference and 95% interval. Then test the corrected net under lookahead2.
- failure modes / confounds: An inference-only swap tests unfamiliar inputs. Accidentally changing the opponent Tera target tests a second intervention. Verify that changing only the other side’s unrevealed type leaves our observation byte-identical.
- formal check: none

## B2: Let a simulated move reveal itself to the leaf evaluator

- lever: search
- generations: gen9
- mechanism: Change one part of lookahead2: when a simulated opponent actually announces a previously unseen move, score the leaf with that move marked revealed and the set posterior recomputed. For example, after surviving a coverage move, the evaluator should know that switching back into it is dangerous. Carry the *observable event* into `LeafEncoder`; do not copy the sampled four-move set or reveal a selected move that never executes. Cache the update by species, prior reveals and emitted move. Keep worlds, depth, gate and value net fixed.
- evidence for: The parity study passed, but its one-turn test found mean absolute win-probability error of **0.043 on reveal transitions versus 0.003 without a reveal** (`ns-10`, P1-B breakdown). The current deployment gate is 0.0025 (`ns-31`, `ns-32`). These quantities are not interchangeable: what matters is whether the error changes the ordering of *actions*. The [Showdown protocol](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/master/sim/SIM-PROTOCOL.md) supplies the reference for what a player observes.
- evidence against / prior attempts: `ns-10` compared against an encoder that deliberately leaves knowledge fields stale between root and leaf; the reveal split also includes items, abilities and species. `ns-21` and `ns-25` spent extra leaves on that same representation. Neither isolates newly announced moves. The uncertain transfer of lookahead2 itself (`ns-20`) argues for a small forecast.
- predicted effect: I infer **+0.8 points, range −0.5 to +2**, over current lookahead2 at fixed 200k. My midpoint assumes roughly one game in ten contains a changed, consequential root choice and that the better choice gains eight points of conditional win probability.
- cost: Estimate 2–3 engineering days; ≤1 T4 GPU-hour for replay validation, then 12–20 slot T4 GPU-hours and 288–480 CPU-hours for head-to-head. No training or main-run risk.
- kill test: First replay fresh self-play transitions against genuine next requests; stop if new-move leaves do not reduce mean absolute value error by ≥20% on that stratum, or if any test reveals an unannounced move. Then candidate versus stock lookahead2 on cd@2030, fixed 1,000 duplicate pairs on the Showdown slot. GO iff the pair-mean 95% lower bound exceeds 50%; void for >2% fallbacks on either side.
- scale-up if GO: Run both variants on identical fresh fixed-200k Foul Play pairs. Test public item consumption separately only after the move-reveal verdict.
- failure modes / confounds: Common value shifts cancel across actions. Misses can still announce moves; full paralysis need not. Copied or called moves must not become evidence of native set membership.
- formal check: none

## B3: Train against the sharper versions of our existing opponents

- lever: opponent/league
- generations: both
- mechanism: Change only the sampling temperature of **frozen and snapshot opponents** from 1.0 to 0.5. Keep the learner at 1.0 during collection, its PPO log probabilities unchanged, ordinary self-play intact, and deployment at 0.5. The experiment asks whether the learner relies on opponents occasionally missing an obvious punish. It costs no extra forward passes and requires no Foul Play decisions.
- evidence for: A colder copy beat the same warm gen4 v1 checkpoint 52.3% [50.9, 53.7] (`v1-19`). In gen9, the raw net scored 53.4% in short games and 64.6% in longer ones at fixed 200k (`dg-08`); I infer that more consistent training opponents might help, not that temperature explains this split. [AlphaStar’s league](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphastar-mastering-the-real-time-strategy-game-starcraft-ii/) is precedent for changing the opponents that supply learning experience, not evidence for this temperature.
- evidence against / prior attempts: The frozen exploiter scored 50.47% [49.05, 51.90] (`ns-30`), but its preregistration §2b says the frozen opponent **trained at T 1.0 and was tested at T 0.5**. It also removed the league. `g9q-22` changed the learner’s entropy incentive. Neither tested sharper opponents inside the original mixture.
- predicted effect: I infer **+0.7 points, range −1 to +2**, for the raw gen9 net at fixed 200k; gen4 is a replication, not an assumed equal gain. I discount the `v1-19` result because only part of training changes, and playing a stronger opponent is not the same as becoming stronger.
- cost: Estimate half an engineering day, 13–16 A40 GPU-hours and 130–160 CPU-hours for both arms. Cluster-side branches in the existing allocation; zero A30 risk.
- kill test: Preregister 30M steps plus identical 5M cooldowns per arm from one gen9 parent, with identical initial opponent files, draw rules and learner settings. In-process candidate versus control, both at T 0.5, fixed 4,000 duplicate pairs. GO iff the 95% lower bound exceeds 50%; log the realized opponent mixture as an integrity check.
- scale-up if GO: Repeat from a second seed; compare to control against our independent pure search and against fixed-200k Foul Play on fresh Showdown pairs. Replicate on a gen4 branch only after transfer.
- failure modes / confounds: Sharpening can make a flawed opponent *more predictable*. Gains against relatives alone would not justify deployment. A change to learner sampling would invalidate the comparison.
- formal check: none

## B4: Learn tactical action differences from the rest of the battle

- lever: training signal
- generations: both
- mechanism: Fork exact fastenv self-play positions *before either player commits*. Compare two legal actions through to the terminal result with frozen continuation policies. Draw the opponent’s simultaneous choice once per replicate from its own view and use it for both actions. Train a small residual on frozen policy logits to predict the **signed difference in wins** from our observation. Keep negative and tied comparisons; do not choose a winning action separately in each hidden world. This can learn both when a setup is a mistake and when sacrificing the active Pokémon is correct.
- evidence for: Low-HP setup while slower and resisted attacks were associated with losses (`dg-02`, `dg-03`, loss report); they are candidates for intervention, not measured recoverable points. The short-game deficit persisted at fixed 200k (`dg-08`). [Showdown’s serializer](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/7b02eb39878f4b486f658a752b38c161379bd352/sim/state.ts) preserves the battle and PRNG state. [Lazaric et al.](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/10-364.html) give the precedent for weighting policy updates by action-value differences.
- evidence against / prior attempts: ExIt’s played-policy targets made the student worse (`ns-27`, `ns-28`); an oracle frequently sided with its own search family in disagreements (`dg-04`). Here neither a teacher’s choice nor our value head supplies the label. Exact terminal outcomes can still be noisy or specific to the continuation policy.
- predicted effect: I infer **+1.2 points, range −1 to +3**, raw gen9 versus matched control at fixed 200k. The midpoint discounts the roughly 2.5 points of combined *association* in the two candidate patterns (`dg-02`); their overlap and reverse causality preclude treating that sum as a causal budget.
- cost: Estimate 3–5 engineering days, 8–20 A16 GPU-hours and 64–160 CPU-hours for the pilot. Stream compact examples; discard cloned states. No main-run change.
- kill test: Preregister 24,000 independent roots, one per game: half uniform, half selected by *pre-action*, player-visible low-HP/setup or resisted-attack opportunity. Compare the sampled move with the most probable distinct legal alternative, eight paired continuations. Fit identical residuals to real differences versus zero differences, anchored to the parent. Test them at T 0.5 over 4,000 fresh, unfiltered in-process duplicate pairs; GO iff the pair-mean 95% lower bound exceeds 50%.
- scale-up if GO: Refresh continuations from a stronger policy, test on gen4, and confirm on Showdown against our search and fixed-200k Foul Play before a second training round.
- failure modes / confounds: Never condition selection on whether the opponent stayed, a later KO, or final game length. Validate both cloned trackers. Replicates sharing a root are not independent games.
- formal check: none

## B5: Make simulated opponents choose from their own information

- lever: search
- generations: gen9
- mechanism: In each of lookahead2’s eight sampled worlds, construct the *opponent’s* first-person observation: its sampled team is known to it; our unused Tera, unseen bench and unrevealed moves are not. Run the frozen general policy once on each such view. At the root, constrain half the opponent’s reply probability to this policy and leave half free to minimize our payoff. Keep our single strategy across worlds, the current gate, and all leaf evaluations. A hidden coverage move should sometimes surprise a strong opponent; a commonly anticipated move should still face a hard reply.
- evidence for: `lookahead2.solve_bayes` minimizes separately in matrices built against our actual private team. That does not model the uncertainty *the opponent* faces. [Jaxcalibur’s author](https://jaxcalibur.github.io/) explicitly addresses this problem by truncating opponent search after surprises. [Johanson et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2007/file/6e7b33fdea3adc80ebd648fffb665bb8-Paper.pdf) describe mixing a fixed response policy with an unrestricted response. Neither source establishes the right mixture here.
- evidence against / prior attempts: Root PUCT and regret matching did not establish gains (`fpr-303472`, `fpr-307076`); sampled root play was harmful (`fpr-307072`). This changes what constrains **their** response, not how we sample ours. `ns-25` used reply priors to allocate extra depth, not this opponent-view constraint at the root.
- predicted effect: I infer **+1.3 points, range −2 to +4**, over stock lookahead2 at fixed 200k. The midpoint assumes a materially different commitment in one game in eight, worth about ten conditional win-probability points. The downside is larger than B2’s: an inaccurate reply prior can erase a valid tactical warning.
- cost: Estimate 4–6 engineering days; 1–2 helper T4 GPU-hours, then 12–20 slot T4 GPU-hours and 288–480 slot CPU-hours. No PPO branch or main-run risk.
- kill test: First require that changing our hidden bench or unused Tera while holding the opponent’s observations and team fixed leaves its prior identical. Then fixed 1,000 Showdown duplicate pairs, candidate versus stock lookahead2 on cd@2030, same eight-world budget and margin. GO iff the pair-mean 95% lower bound exceeds 50%; no mixture sweep after seeing results.
- scale-up if GO: Test against our independent pure search and fixed-200k Foul Play, using matched fresh team pairs per arm. Combine with B2 only in a separately registered incremental comparison; the forecast ranges overlap.
- failure modes / confounds: The unrestricted half still has privileged information. Move-slot mapping, Illusion and first-reveal order can corrupt the reconstructed view. The prior must never consume Foul Play games or depend on opponent identity.
- formal check: For each world with legal reply prior `p`, feasible replies are exactly `y = 0.5p + 0.5z`, where `z` is any distribution over legal replies. Equivalently, `y` is a legal distribution with `yⱼ ≥ 0.5pⱼ` for every reply. This verifies the solver constraint, not a win-rate guarantee.

MEMBER B TOP PICK: B1 — It restores information the player already owns, and the pinned Volcarona sets give a concrete case where the missing type changes what the same move can do.
